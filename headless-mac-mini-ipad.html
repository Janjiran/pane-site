Run a headless Mac mini from your iPad or iPhone

Guide · November 19, 2026

Turn on Screen Sharing on the Mac mini once, and from then on you can control it from your iPad or iPhone with a VNC app such as Pane, with no monitor, keyboard or mouse attached. Sidecar can’t do this: it turns an iPad into a second display for a Mac you are already sitting at, and you start it from the Mac.

First setup needs a screen once

A new or freshly erased Mac mini starts in Setup Assistant with Screen Sharing off, so borrow a TV or monitor with HDMI, a keyboard and a mouse for the first setup.

  1. Go through Setup Assistant and create your user account.
  2. Open System Settings › General › Sharing and turn on Screen Sharing. Click the ⓘ next to it and make sure your user is allowed.
  3. Note the Local hostname at the bottom of the Sharing pane, for example mini.local.
  4. Optionally, turn on Remote Login in the same pane. SSH gives you a second way in if Screen Sharing ever stops answering.

After that you can unplug the borrowed gear and finish setting up from the iPad, or from another Mac with its Screen Sharing app. Connecting from Pane is covered in how to control your Mac from iPhone.

Restarts, FileVault and the login window

Setup Assistant usually turns on FileVault. With FileVault on, a restarted Mac stops at a disk unlock screen before macOS loads. Screen Sharing isn’t running yet, so no VNC app can get you past it, and someone has to type the password at the Mac. For a restart you start yourself, run sudo fdesetup authrestart in Terminal during a Screen Sharing session, and the Mac restarts once without stopping at the unlock screen. A power cut still leaves it waiting there.

With FileVault off, the mini starts to the login window, and Screen Sharing works there: connect with Pane and sign in on the remote login window. Apps that open at login aren’t running yet, though. Pane Relay is one of them, and depending on how you installed it, Tailscale may be too. If you rely on either from outside, a restart while you are away can lock you out until you get home.

Automatic login avoids that. In System Settings › Users & Groups, set Automatically log in as to your account, and the mini comes back to your desktop after any restart. macOS offers this only with FileVault off, and the cost is physical security: anyone who walks off with the mini gets your files and every app you are signed in to. That can be acceptable in a locked home office. In a shared space, keep FileVault on and unlock it by hand after a restart.

Screen resolution without a display

With no display attached, macOS falls back to a default resolution for Screen Sharing, and System Settings › Displays may offer few other choices. If text on the iPad looks too small or soft, get an HDMI dummy plug, a small adapter sold as a headless display emulator that makes the Mac believe a monitor is connected, and choose a resolution in System Settings › Displays. An iPad screen is closer to 4:3 than most monitors, so a 16:10 resolution leaves less empty border than 16:9.

If you connect and see a black screen or only a login window, the mini is locked or another user is logged in. Sign in on the remote login window. macOS may ask whether to share the current display or open a virtual display for you.

Keep it awake

On a Mac mini these switches are in System Settings › Energy (Energy Saver on macOS 13 Ventura):

  • Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off keeps the mini reachable at all times. Pane Relay and Tailscale need that, because neither can wake a sleeping Mac.
  • Wake for network access lets Pane wake the mini when you are on the same network. See wake a sleeping Mac from iPhone.
  • Start up automatically after a power failure brings the mini back after an outage, though with FileVault on it then waits at the unlock screen.

Reach it from outside your home

Your router blocks connections that start on the internet. Use one of these instead of forwarding a port:

  • Tailscale, free for personal use, on the mini and on the iPad or iPhone, signed in to the same account. Put the mini’s Tailscale name under Away from home in Pane, and turn off key expiry for the mini in the Tailscale admin console, or it drops off your network when its key expires (after 180 days by default). Full steps: Mac Screen Sharing over Tailscale.
  • Pane Relay, a menu-bar app for the mini, $19 a year after a 14-day free trial. You normally pair it by scanning a code in the Mac’s menu bar, which is awkward when the only view of that menu bar is on your iPhone. Copy the code instead: with Shared clipboard on, open Pane Relay’s menu during a Pane session, click Copy pairing code, and paste it under Pane Relay on the Mac’s Edit screen in Pane.

Remote desktop without port forwarding compares these with the other options.

iPad specifics

On iPad, Pane shows a session panel beside the Mac’s screen with latency, frame rate, bandwidth, the current display and Quality. If the session feels slow, check latency there and set Quality to Fast. Tap 1/2 at the top to step to the next display, then to all of them side by side.

With a hardware keyboard attached, the keyboard button at the right of the dock decides whether keys go to the Mac or stay on the iPad. iPadOS keeps a few shortcuts for itself, including ⌘H, ⌘Tab and ⌘Space, so send those with the modifier keys in the dock. Under Settings for this Mac, turn on Lock Mac on disconnect so the mini locks when you end a session.

The Window view and Workspace list that Pane Relay adds work only on iPhone for now. On iPad you get the full desktop.

Wake a sleeping Mac from iPhone and connect

Guide · November 19, 2026

Turn on Wake for network access on the Mac and give Pane the Mac’s hardware (MAC) address. Pane’s Wake button then sends a Wake-on-LAN packet that brings the Mac out of sleep, and you connect as usual. This works only while the iPhone is on the same local network as the Mac, and only from sleep: a Mac that is shut down can’t be woken over the network.

Sleep and shut down are different

A sleeping Mac keeps its network hardware listening, so a wake packet can reach it. A Mac that is shut down has nothing listening, and Macs can’t be powered on over the network. To keep a Mac within reach, let it sleep or leave it awake. On a desktop Mac, also turn on Start up automatically after a power failure in System Settings › Energy, so an outage doesn’t leave it off.

Turn on Wake for network access

  • Mac mini, iMac, Mac Studio or Mac Pro: System Settings › Energy › Wake for network access. On macOS 13 Ventura the pane is called Energy Saver.
  • MacBook: System Settings › Battery › Options › Wake for network access. Choose Always, or Only on Power Adapter if the Mac stays plugged in.

On macOS 12 and earlier the same option is in System Preferences, under Energy Saver or Battery.

Ethernet or Wi-Fi

Wake-on-LAN is most dependable over Ethernet. Many Macs can also wake over Wi-Fi, though how well depends on the Mac and the router. If you plan to wake a desktop Mac regularly, plug it into the router with a cable.

An Apple TV or HomePod on your network can act as a Bonjour Sleep Proxy. It keeps announcing the sleeping Mac’s services and wakes the Mac when a connection comes in. That is why Pane can sometimes list a sleeping Mac under Macs on this network as asleep, wakes on connect.

Find the hardware address

On the Mac, open System Settings › Network, choose Ethernet or Wi-Fi, click Details and open Hardware. The MAC address looks like aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff. Ethernet and Wi-Fi each have their own address, so copy the one for the connection the Mac uses while it sleeps. If you switch the Mac from Wi-Fi to Ethernet later, update the address in Pane.

Set it up in Pane

  1. In Pane, open the Mac’s details, tap Edit and enter the hardware address under Wake-on-LAN.
  2. When the Mac is asleep, the home screen shows a Wake button on it. Tap it, and the button shows Waking… until the Mac answers.
  3. Or just tap the Mac to connect. If it doesn’t answer, the connecting screen offers Wake & retry.

Behind the button, Pane sends a standard Wake-on-LAN magic packet as a broadcast on the network your iPhone is on. The Mac’s network hardware recognizes its own address in the packet and wakes the Mac, and Pane then connects to Screen Sharing as usual. Because it is a local broadcast, Pane needs the Local Network permission for this. If you turned it off, see Local Network permission.

From outside your home

Wake-on-LAN packets are broadcasts, and routers don’t pass broadcasts in from the internet. Tailscale and Pane Relay can’t help here, because both need the Mac awake to answer. For a Mac you need while you are away:

  • Keep it awake. On a desktop Mac, turn on Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off in System Settings › Energy. The display can still turn off.
  • Use your router, if it can send a wake packet from its own app or web page. Some can. Wake the Mac that way, then connect.
  • Use another computer that is always on at home, such as a Raspberry Pi or a NAS you can reach over Tailscale, to send the packet with a command line tool like wakeonlan.

Keeping the Mac awake is the simplest of these, since it needs nothing else at home. To choose how to reach it, see remote desktop without port forwarding.

Troubleshooting

What happensWhat to check
Wake does nothingThere is no hardware address under Wake-on-LAN, Wake for network access is off, the Mac is on a different network, or it is shut down.
Works on Ethernet, not on Wi-FiWi-Fi wake depends on the Mac and the router. Use Ethernet for a Mac you wake often.
Nothing wakes from a guest networkGuest Wi-Fi usually keeps devices apart from the main network, so the broadcast never reaches the Mac. Join the main network.
It wakes, then Pane says Can’t reachGive the Mac a few seconds and tap Try again. If it still fails, check that Screen Sharing is on and the Mac’s firewall allows it.
It stopped working after a network changeThe address in Pane belongs to the other connection. Copy the hardware address again from System Settings › Network.

The full list of error messages is on the support page.

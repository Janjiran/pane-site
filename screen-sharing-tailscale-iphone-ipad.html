Mac Screen Sharing over Tailscale from iPhone and iPad

Guide · November 19, 2026

Install Tailscale on the Mac and on your iPhone or iPad, sign in to the same tailnet on both, and the Mac’s Screen Sharing is reachable at its Tailscale name or 100.x address from any network. Tailscale encrypts the connection with WireGuard, which also covers the parts of a VNC session that are not encrypted on their own.

Pane and Understudios are not affiliated with Tailscale.

Set up both devices

Screen Sharing has to be on first (System Settings › General › Sharing). If it isn’t, follow how to control your Mac from iPhone and test it on your home Wi-Fi before adding Tailscale.

  1. Install Tailscale on the Mac from tailscale.com/download and sign in.
  2. Make sure Tailscale starts when the Mac does. Turn on its launch at login option, or add it in System Settings › General › Login Items (Login Items & Extensions on macOS 15 and later).
  3. Install Tailscale on the iPhone or iPad from the App Store and sign in to the same tailnet.
  4. Leave the VPN connected on the iPhone. When it is off, the Mac’s Tailscale address doesn’t answer, and Pane reports Can’t reach.
  5. In the Tailscale admin console, check that MagicDNS is on under DNS. Newer tailnets have it on by default.

Which address to use

Pane accepts any of the three ways Tailscale names your Mac:

  • The short MagicDNS name, such as studio, which is the machine name shown in the admin console.
  • The full name, such as studio.tail1234.ts.net. Use it if the short name doesn’t resolve on your phone.
  • The 100.x.y.z address. It stays the same for as long as the Mac is in your tailnet, and works even with MagicDNS off.

The Tailscale app on the iPhone lists your devices with their names and addresses.

Add it in Pane

For a Mac you already use at home, open its details in Pane, tap Edit and enter the Tailscale name or address under Away from home. Pane tries the home address first and the remote address when the home one doesn’t answer, and the connecting screen also has a Try remote address button. At home you keep the local connection, and anywhere else the same saved Mac works over Tailscale.

You can also add a Mac with only its Tailscale address: tap Connect to a Mac and type the name or 100.x address into Address. The port stays 5900. Sign in with your Mac user name and password as usual. Connections over Tailscale don’t use the iPhone’s Local Network permission, so they work even if you turned it off.

Custom access controls

The default Tailscale policy lets your devices reach each other, so most people can skip this. If you have edited the policy file, a rule must allow your iPhone or iPad to reach the Mac on TCP port 5900. In the grants syntax that is tcp:5900. In the older ACL syntax it is port 5900 on the Mac as the destination. Without that rule both devices show as connected in Tailscale, and Pane still can’t reach the Mac.

Direct and relayed connections

Tailscale tries to connect your devices directly. When the network on either side gets in the way, traffic goes through Tailscale’s DERP relay servers instead. A relayed connection works, but the extra hop adds latency and limits speed, and screen sharing feels it. Cellular networks often sit behind carrier-grade NAT, which makes direct connections harder, so the session can feel slower on cellular than on Wi-Fi.

To check, run tailscale status in Terminal on the Mac and look at the iPhone’s line, or run tailscale ping followed by the iPhone’s name. A reply “via DERP” means relayed. An IP address and port means direct. Allowing UPnP or NAT-PMP on your home router can help Tailscale find a direct path. In Pane, setting Quality to Fast helps on a slow link, and on iPad the session panel shows the latency.

Encryption

On its own, VNC to the Mac encrypts the login and not the screen, keystrokes or clipboard (see Is the connection encrypted?). Over Tailscale the whole session runs inside WireGuard between the iPhone and the Mac, so you can use it on public Wi-Fi.

If it stops working

  • The Tailscale VPN is off on the iPhone. Open the app and connect.
  • Tailscale isn’t running on the Mac, for example after a restart if the Mac is waiting at the login window. Whether it connects before anyone logs in depends on how you installed it.
  • The Mac’s node key expired. Keys expire after 180 days by default. For a Mac that stays home, turn off key expiry for it in the admin console.
  • The Mac is asleep. Tailscale can’t wake it, and Wake-on-LAN works only on the local network. See wake a sleeping Mac from iPhone.

When Tailscale doesn’t fit

iOS runs one VPN at a time, so Tailscale competes with a work VPN or any other VPN app you need. On a managed phone your company may not let you add a VPN at all. Some people also don’t want another account, or don’t want to install a VPN on a family member’s devices.

In those cases Pane Relay is the alternative: a menu-bar app for the Mac that you pair by scanning a code, with no account, and it doesn’t use the iPhone’s VPN slot. It costs $19 a year per Mac after a 14-day free trial. It is not encrypted end to end today, and it carries only Pane sessions. Remote desktop without port forwarding compares all the options.

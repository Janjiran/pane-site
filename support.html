1. Turn on Screen Sharing on your Mac

Pane talks to the Screen Sharing service built into macOS, so there is nothing to install on the Mac.

  1. Open System Settings › General › Sharing.
  2. Turn on Screen Sharing. (If Remote Management is already on, Screen Sharing is included in it; Pane works with both.)
  3. Click the ⓘ next to Screen Sharing and choose who may connect: All users or Only these users.
  4. Optional: under Computer Settings, enable VNC viewers may control screen with password only if you want to sign in with a separate VNC password instead of your Mac account. Pane detects which one the Mac asks for.
  5. Note the Mac’s name shown under General › About (for example Studio). On your local network it is reachable as studio.local.

In Pane, tap Connect to a Mac at the bottom of the home screen (on iPad: New connection in the top bar). Macs with Screen Sharing on appear under Macs on this network within a few seconds; tap one, or type the .local name or IP address into Address. Port 5900 and your Mac account are assumed; Advanced holds Port, Quality (Auto · Sharp · Fast) and Displays (All · Main). Tap Connect, then sign in with your Mac user name and Password (the same ones you use at the login screen). Leave Save to Keychain on and Face ID unlocks it next time.

Let the Mac wake up

So that Pane can wake a sleeping Mac, enable Wake for network access in System Settings › Battery › Options (laptops) or Energy (desktops), and keep the Mac on Ethernet or the same Wi-Fi network. Then, in Pane, open the Mac’s details, tap Edit and enter its hardware address under Wake-on-LAN (on the Mac: System Settings › Network › Wi-Fi or Ethernet › Details › Hardware). An asleep Mac then shows a Wake button on the home screen, and the connecting screen offers Wake & retry. Waking works on the local network only.

2. Connect from anywhere with Tailscale

Tailscale gives each of your devices a private, stable address that works from any network, with no port forwarding.

  1. Install Tailscale on the Mac (tailscale.com/download) and on your iPhone or iPad (App Store), and sign in to the same account on both.
  2. In the Tailscale admin console, make sure MagicDNS is on. Your Mac is then reachable by its machine name, for example studio or studio.tail1234.ts.net, or by its 100.x.y.z address.
  3. In Pane, open the Mac’s details, tap Edit and enter that name or address under Away from home. When the Mac does not answer on its home address, the connecting screen offers Try remote address. (You can also add a new Mac with the Tailscale address directly; the port stays 5900.)
  4. Make sure the Tailscale app is connected on your iPhone or iPad before you connect. Tailscale on the Mac must be running too; set it to start at login.

Tailscale encrypts the whole connection end to end, so this is also the recommended way to use Pane on public Wi-Fi.

4. Gestures in a session

The dock’s trackpad button switches between Trackpad (the screen is a trackpad for the Mac’s pointer, the default) and Touch (your finger is the pointer). The controls fade after a few seconds; bring them back without clicking anything with a three-finger tap, or by tapping the black area around the Mac’s screen.

5. Troubleshooting

“Pane would like to paste from…”

With Shared clipboard on (the default), Pane mirrors what you copy on the iPhone or iPad to the Mac, and iOS asks before any app reads text you copied in another app. Tap Allow Paste. To stop the prompt for good, open Settings › Apps › Pane › Paste from Other Apps and choose Allow (on iOS 17 and earlier: Settings › Pane). The same prompt appears when you tap the paste button on the New connection screen. If you would rather not share the clipboard, turn it off under Settings for this Mac.

Local Network permission

The first time Pane scans for Macs or connects on your Wi-Fi, iOS asks to allow Local Network access (“Pane looks for computers with Screen Sharing on your network and connects to them.”). If you tapped Don’t Allow, Macs on this network stays empty and .local names and local IP addresses fail with Can’t reach. Turn it on in Settings › Apps › Pane › Local Network (or Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network), then tap Scan again. Connections over Tailscale do not use the local network and are not affected.

Is the connection encrypted?

Pane talks VNC directly to your Mac. Your login is encrypted; the screen, keystrokes and clipboard are not, so use it on your own Wi-Fi or over Tailscale or a VPN when away. Section 2 above explains how to set Tailscale up; it encrypts the whole connection end to end.

Can I turn off analytics?

Yes. You can turn usage analytics off in the app under About (Home › sliders button). Events already sent are not affected; nothing is sent after the switch is off. What is collected is listed in the privacy policy.
